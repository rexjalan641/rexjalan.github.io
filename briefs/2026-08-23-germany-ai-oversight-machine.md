## 1. Why this matters (≤120 words)

Germany has switched on its national machinery for enforcing the EU AI Act. The KI-MIG, the Act on the Market Surveillance and Support of Innovation of Artificial Intelligence, entered into force on 29 July 2026. It makes the Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA), Germany's network regulator, the country's central AI authority: market surveillance authority, single point of contact, and complaints hub for the AI Act. But Germany did not build a single super-regulator. Financial-sector AI stays with the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin), media with the state media authorities, and a new Coordination and Competence Centre for the AI Regulation (KoKIVO) is meant to hold the patchwork together. If you want to track how the AI Act bites in Europe's largest market, this is the architecture to watch.

## 3. Curated excerpts with EN translation

### Excerpt 1: The handover is immediate

> **DE original:**
> "Die Bundesnetzagentur übernimmt ab sofort eine zentrale Rolle bei der Umsetzung der europäischen KI-Verordnung in Deutschland. Diese ergibt sich aus dem „Gesetz zur Marktüberwachung und Innovationsförderung von künstlicher Intelligenz" (KI-MIG), welches heute in Kraft tritt."

**EN (working translation — AI-assisted, reviewed by the author):**
"With immediate effect, the Bundesnetzagentur takes on a central role in the implementation of the European AI Regulation in Germany. This results from the 'Act on the Market Surveillance and Support of Innovation of Artificial Intelligence' (KI-MIG), which enters into force today."

**Annotation:** Two things packed into one sentence. First, timing: there was no gap between the law passing and the authority acting. BNetzA announced its new powers on the same day the KI-MIG entered into force (both 29 July 2026). Second, naming: the statute's full title pairs *Marktüberwachung* (market surveillance) with *Innovationsförderung* (innovation promotion), so enforcement and sandbox-style support are legally twin mandates, not an add-on. Note what the release does not say: no staffing numbers, no budget, no interim milestones between now and the AI Act's high-risk obligations applying from December 2027 (per Omnibus Regulation (EU) 2026/1744).

### Excerpt 2: The president's framing — enabler, not enforcer

> **DE original:**
> "Die KI-Verordnung ist ein Meilenstein für den verantwortungsvollen Einsatz von Künstlicher Intelligenz in Europa", sagt Klaus Müller, Präsident der Bundesnetzagentur. "Die Bundesnetzagentur wird in ihrer neuen Rolle sowohl die Sicherheit und den Schutz der Grundrechte gewährleisten als auch Innovationen fördern. Wir verstehen uns als Enabler und Ansprechpartner für das gesamte KI-Ökosystem."

**EN:** Official BNetzA English edition:
"The AI Act is a milestone for the responsible use of artificial intelligence in Europe … In its new role, the Bundesnetzagentur will ensure security and protection of basic rights as well as promoting innovation. We see ourselves as an enabler and point of contact for the whole AI ecosystem."

**Annotation:** Klaus Müller (already one of Germany's most visible regulators from the energy price crisis) frames the agency's self-image deliberately around "Enabler" (the English word is used in the German original). For an English reader this signals the posture SMEs and start-ups can expect: guidance-first, with the AI Service Desk and regulatory sandboxes foregrounded before any mention of enforcement mechanics. It is positioning, not a legal commitment; the statute's enforcement powers are separate and considerably less cuddly.

### Excerpt 3: The hybrid model — expertise decides who watches

> **DE original:**
> "Die Umsetzung der KI-Verordnung erfolgt in einem hybriden Ansatz, der die Aufsicht nach Sachnähe aufteilt und Doppelstrukturen vermeidet und eine kohärente Auslegung der KI-Verordnung sicherstellt. In vollharmonisierten Produktbereichen bleiben die etablierten Marktüberwachungs- und Fachaufsichtsbehörden zuständig, um Unternehmen die bekannten Ansprechpartner zu erhalten, etwa die Bundesanstalt für Finanzdienstleistungsaufsicht im Finanzsektor oder die Landesmedienanstalten im Medienbereich." *(BNetzA press release, 29 Jul 2026)*

**EN (working translation — AI-assisted, reviewed by the author):**
"Implementation of the AI Regulation follows a hybrid approach that divides oversight by proximity to subject matter (*Sachnähe*), avoids duplicate structures, and ensures a coherent interpretation of the AI Regulation. In fully harmonised product sectors, the established market surveillance and specialist supervisory authorities remain competent so that companies keep their familiar contact persons — for example the Federal Financial Supervisory Authority (BaFin) in the financial sector or the state media authorities in the media sector."

**Annotation:** This is the sentence that kills the "German AI super-regulator" narrative. BNetzA is the *default* authority, not the *exclusive* one: sectors that already have sector-specific supervisors under harmonised EU product law keep them, so companies deal with supervisors they know. The statute confirms this division of labour in § 2: BNetzA covers AI in radio equipment and high-risk systems in sensitive areas such as employment, critical infrastructure and education (§ 2(1)), while § 2(3) routes financial-sector AI to BaFin's existing mandate and § 2(6)/(8) leave state-law authorities competent for public bodies and media services. The press release adds that federal states may use BNetzA via *Organleihe* (borrowing of the federal agency). The stated rationale is avoiding duplication and ensuring consistent interpretation. It also means accountability for AI harms is spread across a dozen authorities rather than concentrated in one address.

### Excerpt 4: KoKIVO — the coordination layer holding the patchwork together

*(KoKIVO = the Coordination and Competence Centre for the AI Regulation, introduced in §1)*

> **DE original:**
> "In Deutschland sollen Wissen und Ressourcen der KI-Verordnung an zentraler Stelle gebündelt werden, um insbesondere die nationalen Marktüberwachungs- und notifizierenden Behörden des Bundes sowie der Bundesländer als Service- und Beratungsstelle bei der komplexen Anwendung der KI-Verordnung zu unterstützen." *(BNetzA KoKIVO page)*

**EN (working translation — AI-assisted, reviewed by the author):**
"In Germany, knowledge and resources on the AI Regulation are to be bundled in one central place, in order to support in particular the national market surveillance and notifying authorities of the federation and the federal states as a service and advisory point in the complex application of the AI Regulation."

**Annotation:** The Koordinierungs- und Kompetenzzentrum für die KI-Verordnung (Coordination and Competence Centre for the AI Regulation; *KoKIVO*: keep the acronym, there's no clean English equivalent) sits inside BNetzA but serves the *other* watchdogs. Per § 5 KI-MIG its jobs include: advising authorities on complex decisions on request, coordinating cooperation so horizontal legal questions get uniform answers, facilitating codes of conduct under Art. 95(2) of the AI Act, ensuring civil society, unions, business and academia are appropriately involved, and providing training materials. The page hedges: tasks are listed as "voraussichtlich" (prospective/expected). Read together with Excerpt 3, Germany chose coordination-by-expertise-pool over consolidation, betting that shared technical knowledge can substitute for a single powerful regulator. Whether that holds under the first cross-sector enforcement cases is exactly what to watch.

### Excerpt 5: One door for complaints — and an independent decision chamber

> **DE original:**
> "Beschwerden wegen eines Verstoßes gegen die Verordnung (EU) 2024/1689 können unbeschadet der Vorgaben des Artikels 85 der Verordnung (EU) 2024/1689 bei der Bundesnetzagentur als zentraler Beschwerdestelle eingereicht werden." *(§ 8 Abs. 1 KI-MIG)*
>
> "Die KI-Marktüberwachungskammer handelt völlig unabhängig. Sie unterliegt weder direkter noch indirekter Beeinflussung von außen …" *(§ 4 Abs. 3 KI-MIG)*

**EN (working translation — AI-assisted, reviewed by the author):**
"Complaints concerning a violation of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 may be submitted to the Bundesnetzagentur as the central complaints office, without prejudice to the requirements of Article 85 of Regulation (EU) 2024/1689." *(§ 8(1))*
"The AI Market Surveillance Chamber acts with complete independence. It is subject to neither direct nor indirect external influence …" *(§ 4(3))*

**Annotation:** Two structural details worth knowing. (1) Anyone can file an AI Act complaint at one federal address regardless of which of the many sectoral authorities ends up competent. BNetzA triages and forwards (§ 8(2)), and must tell complainants where their case went. (2) Actual enforcement decisions sit with a three-member KI-Marktüberwachungskammer chaired by the BNetzA president, which the statute insulates from external influence and which must report annually to the Bundestag (first report due 31 March 2027, covering 2026). That first annual report will be the earliest hard evidence of whether this machine actually enforces.

## 5. Open questions

- **Resourcing:** The RegE cost assessment (BMDS, Feb 2026) plans ~43 posts and ~€6.23M/yr for BNetzA (33.2 specialist + 9.8 overhead posts; personnel €3.678M, materials €1.137M, overhead €1.416M), plus €3M one-off and €6.3M/yr for IT, the KI-Reallabor and the EU database interface. Also itemized: BSI 13 posts (~€2.5M/yr), ZITsiS 8 posts (~€1.48M/yr), BfDI ~€1M/yr, BAuA 5 posts (~€995K/yr from 2027). Figures are planning estimates pending annual budget procedures — "central authority" capacity is still untested in practice.
- **Länder delegation:** Which federal states, if any, will use *Organleihe* (borrowing of the federal agency) to place AI supervision with BNetzA? Watch the BNetzA AI pages and state justice/digital ministries.
- **KoKIVO final shape:** Tasks are still "voraussichtlich" (expected) — monitor the KoKIVO page for a founding document replacing the hedge.
- **First annual report:** The KI-Marktüberwachungskammer's report for 2026 is due to the Bundestag by 31 March 2027 — the first test of whether the oversight machine moves.
- **BaFin specifics:** BaFin (Federal Financial Supervisory Authority) is named in the PR only in passing; watch its own publications for how its AI mandate will operate in practice (supervisory notices, guidance for regulated institutions).
