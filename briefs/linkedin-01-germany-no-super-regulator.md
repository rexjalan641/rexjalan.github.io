Germany switched on its national AI Act enforcement machinery on 29 July. The interesting part is the design choice behind it.

Most assumed every EU member state would build the same centralized AI agency. Germany took a different path: the Bundesnetzagentur became the central authority and single point of contact, while financial-sector AI stays with BaFin, media with the state media authorities, and a new coordination centre holds the system together.

The reasoning matches how AI actually shows up in the economy — embedded in banking, medical devices, broadcasting and employment — so Germany bet on regulators who already know each sector, supported by a shared pool of AI expertise.

Every EU member state now faces the same design choice. Germany is the largest market to answer it.
