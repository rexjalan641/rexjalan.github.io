About

I'm a student of International Relations at the Catholic University of Eichstätt-Ingolstadt. I study how artificial intelligence is entering statecraft: the strategies governments write, the institutions they build, and the deals they make.

AI policy is often covered in separate pieces. Regulation sits here, chips there, summits somewhere else. In practice these areas are connected, and my work follows those connections. One project is a source-linked tracker of how Germany implements and enforces the EU AI Act; from there, my writing extends to the wider international picture.

Coverage

I publish one deep piece at a time. Each takes on a single question and stays with it; over the months, the questions add up to the full field.

01

Models & capability

When a new system does something new, I ask who gains power from it. Frontier releases, open weights, evaluation, military adoption.

02

AI diplomacy & global governance

Where governments meet over AI: summits, UN and G7 processes, national institutes for AI safety, and the fight over who writes global standards.

03

National strategies

What Washington, Beijing, Brussels, Berlin, and Delhi actually promise, fund, and build, read against what they deliver.

04

Regulation & implementation

The EU AI Act as it touches the real world, and Germany's enforcement machine in particular. My tracker documents this area.

05

Hardware & supply chains

The physical base of AI: fabs in Dresden and Taiwan, ASML's monopoly, export controls, compute as a strategic resource.

06

Trade & economic security

Chip wars, sanctions, investment screening, and what AI does to jobs and competitiveness in exporting economies.

07

Partnerships & alliances

Countries picking their technology partners. Includes the India–Germany corridor, which I track closely.

08

Security & defence

Military AI, NATO posture, arms control questions, and cybersecurity.
