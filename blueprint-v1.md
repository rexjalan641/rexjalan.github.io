## Distribution rails (build now, activate gradually — approved 23 Aug)
Principle: one production pipeline, many outlets. Every piece originates as a brief/tracker entry, then adapts outward. No platform gets original effort beyond adaptation.
- **Substack (PRIMARY NOW)**: F-briefs home. Free tier only. Email list starts day 1 = the monetization asset.
- **LinkedIn**: 1 post/week adapted from F/I content. Fresh German LinkedIn account pending move.
- **Static site**: anchor tracker + evergreen archive. NOT live until Impressum address + settled in Eichstätt.
- **X/Twitter (LATER)**: dormant account ok; activate only when there's a backlog of 10+ pieces to link.
- **YouTube (2027+, conditional)**: explainer versions of I-memos or syllabus-style content ONLY IF (a) Werkstudent income is stable, (b) list ≥500 subs, (c) editing can be batched monthly. Video is a time sink at 10 hrs/wk — treat as year-2 option, not a pillar.
- **Other forums** (Mastodon/policy Discord/Reddit r/eupersonalfinance-adjacent policy subs): participate as a reader/commenter now; zero posting obligations.
- Monetization sequence: Werkstudent salary (income #1) → paid Substack tier or paid deep-dives mid-2027 if list ≥500 → courses/workshops only after German credential + audience. No billing setup before then.

## Devil's Advocate tripwires (built-in accountability)
1. ZERO-VISIBILITY: public v0 of the tracker must exist by end of Sept 2026 (ugly GitHub Pages counts). Miss it → drop static-site premise, distribute via LinkedIn posts + shared doc only.
2. SPRAWL: if >2 projects have work-in-progress simultaneously by week 6, freeze everything except the anchor.
3. COLLISION: any two consecutive weeks below ~5 hrs on the anchor (exam/application season Nov–Dec) → pre-publish "paused until Feb" notice and shift hours to applications. Planned degradation, not failure.
4. OUTREACH RATIO: ≥30% of weekly career-hours go to direct outreach/applications starting immediately — the portfolio exists to make those emails less awkward, not to postpone them.
